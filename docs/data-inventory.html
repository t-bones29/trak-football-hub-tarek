01  Before relying on this inventory

Open decisions and source discrepancies

The academy has not been briefed

A meeting is planned in the coming weeks. Scope, data responsibilities, notices, retention, suppliers, calendar exposure and recovery limitations have not been accepted by the academy. Counsel decisions and operational confirmations remain open.

Admission begins before in-app approval

Trak's operator loads the supplied roster, including DOB and guardian contacts. Coaches cannot create new squad rows. The 8 October product decision permits the coach to view the academy-supplied player profile before approval; assessments remain blocked until approval. The roster is treated as academy data in this flow. Guardian approval can precede child account creation; the initial transfer still needs an agreed basis, notice and responsibility. Email confirmation and a declared relationship are technical evidence, not a legal finding of parental authority.

The consent choice and access control differ

The routed screen requires coaching_records and offers recognition and parent_visibility as optional. Database approval checks use active coaching consent; a false parent-visibility choice does not independently block linked-parent history reads. Recognition authoring is separately parked. Align the screen, enforcement and final notice before relying on the approval.

Classification and retention need answers

Dropping wellness does not establish a legal classification for assessments, free text or retained data, or settle whether an impact assessment is required. Retention periods remain open. Do not read invitation expiry or a stale-consent report as automatic erasure.

J8 is a launch requirement, still to implement

The confirmed J8 requirements include weekly events, kit, fixture import, parent absence reports, personal calendar links, event emails, manual sharing and completed registers for the first real-child pilot. The legacy calendar below is parked for app authoring. Its existence does not demonstrate the new J8 flow or calendar-link controls.

02  Current source

Public tables and access boundaries

These are schema categories and code controls, not claims that every field is populated in production. “Family” means the child or their linked parent where that table permits access. Ordinary family development-record reads and new development writes require active approval below 18; unknown age does not grant a bypass. Retained player-logged matches have an explicit read exception. Account export uses a separate path described in section 05. This is the implemented threshold, not a legal conclusion. Trusted operator access is additional to app access.

03  Beyond public tables

Authentication, files and suppliers

Supabase Auth and email

Auth processes identities, credentials and sessions. For a child without email, the guardian-created username maps to an internal address under child.trakfootball.com; this is not a mailbox. Create/reset handlers pass passwords to Auth without storing them in application tables or returning them. Reset attempts to end existing child sessions and reports a partial outcome if that step fails.

Roster emails carry the recipient address and invitation context, including child first name and organization name. Auth's invitation and sign-in APIs deliver them. The actual Auth delivery provider, dashboard templates, expiry settings, message/log retention and processing regions need operational confirmation. J8 also requires a general event-email sender; its provider, region and from address are open in TRAK-126.

Hosting and monitoring

Vercel hosts the web app. Supabase provides database, Auth, storage and Edge Functions. Sentry initializes only in production with a configured DSN; browser tracing samples at 10%, and no session-replay integration is configured. Error and request data need a declared scope and retention period.

Regions, actual service configuration and transfer arrangements are unverified here. Supplier headquarters and hostname hints do not establish where processing occurs.

Historical media and AI

The pilot blocks avatar reads, uploads and changes by app roles, and blocks new avatar URLs. Historical storage objects, previously issued links and cached copies require separate review. The three older AI handlers return PILOT_FEATURE_DISABLED; the old gateway is not active pilot processing in this source baseline.

Operator files and recovery

Roster files, support correspondence, exports, correction evidence and backup/restore material can exist outside application tables. Their storage, access and deletion procedures remain to be agreed.

Operating record: TRAK-23, 2 October 2026, records daily backups retained for seven days, no point-in-time recovery (PITR) and an unrehearsed restore. The current pilot decision also excludes staging. These arrangements have not been reverified in the dashboard. The restore document must state coverage, evidence and limitations. The academy has not yet been briefed.

Calendar privacy requirements are specified

Withdrawal, departure or revocation must stop child data on the next feed request, including affected extra links. Regenerating a personal link revokes the old one. This does not promise immediate deletion from provider/device caches. Last fetched records a request, not a reader's identity or understanding. Time and venue disclose where a squad trains even without child names.

Counsel must see the feed privacy design before real families receive links. One-tap setup has an easy skip and Settings access later. Apple, Google and Outlook subscription behaviour must be proved on real devices; direct provider-account connections are outside J8.

05  Lifecycle and legal review

Withdrawal, deletion and export have different scopes

Withdrawal and retention

Withdrawal blocks covered development writes and hides covered content through ordinary family read policies while approval is required. Retained player-logged matches have a read exception, and account export does not apply the same consent predicate. Withdrawal does not erase data; reapproval can restore ordinary visibility. Consent evidence intentionally outlives roster/account deletion. Coach departure also preserves selected history while closing former-coach access.

Set periods for each category, including unclaimed rosters, notes, consent, telemetry, correction audit, backups and external files. Invitation expiry and a stale-consent review report do not supply a general retention policy.

Account deletion

delete_my_account() removes many linked records and triggers cascades, including the user's telemetry. It preserves some coach history and does not delete consent evidence. It cannot by itself establish removal of supplier logs, backups, email, calendar caches or operator files. Do not claim complete erasure or a current successful rehearsal from source inspection.

Account export

export_my_account() exists and returns data scoped to the caller's role. The source switch includes coach observations about a player. Parent export includes the parent's own links/consents, not a complete child export. The child's application export excludes private coach notes.

The export function executes with elevated database privileges; its player branch reads own matches, assessments and awards without the family consent predicate. Source logic therefore retains that export access after withdrawal. Whether this is intended, and how it should be explained alongside withdrawal and rights handling, needs an engineering/counsel decision. No live test is asserted.

The latest definition predates roster/child-login additions and omits those categories, shared coach messages and training attendance. Its software scope does not determine the scope of a reviewed access or portability request.
